The college

Where is Bowles Hall located?

Bowles Hall is on campus, on Stadium Rim Way between the Greek Theatre and California Memorial Stadium. See it on a map.

What is a residential college?

There are roughly 130 residential colleges in the U.S. and nearly 500 worldwide. First established at Oxford in 1249, a residential college is typically a permanent, cross-sectional, faculty-led residence that offers the advantages of a small community within a large university.

Social and cultural programming is designed and run by the student residents, with faculty and other experienced adults alongside them. That self-governance gives residents real leadership experience. Academics matter, but it is best thought of as a home away from home. As Jonathan Holloway of Yale's Calhoun College put it, it's where “adults live alongside the students, celebrating their successes and helping them navigate their challenges.”

Who is behind the Bowles Hall Residential College?

The Bowles Hall Foundation, a privately funded non-profit, operates the Hall. Bowles opened in 1929 as the country's first residential college, but over the decades its mission and living standards faded. A group of alumni led by Bob Sayles ('52) spent ten years restoring its legacy.

The Foundation's focus is forward-looking: helping shape meaningful contributors to society. We believe students benefit from living in community with peers, faculty and alumni, and from structured academic, social and career-readiness support across their whole undergraduate career.

Does Cal Housing run the facility?

No. Bowles Hall is privately owned and operated under a 45-year ground lease the Foundation signed with the University of California after approval by the Regents. Its $45 million renovation was funded by private revenue bonds, with no funds from the University, the UC system or California taxpayers. We work closely with the administration to follow standard policies, and several Cal faculty and administrators serve on our Board.

What's the difference between the Bowles Hall Foundation and the Bowles Hall Association?

The Foundation is the non-profit that operates the Hall. The Bowles Hall Association is the formal name of the student government.

What role do the in-residence faculty play?

Three non-undergraduate roles mentor students as they choose majors, courses, projects and activities, and plan for life after Cal:

  • College Principal: a Berkeley faculty member responsible for the college's academic, intellectual, social, athletic and cultural development.
  • College Dean: also Berkeley faculty, focused on helping residents reach their academic goals.
  • Director of Program Development: keeps programming in balance with students' changing needs.

All of them live and dine among students. They also run mentoring programs in which upperclass residents help younger residents, grouped by shared academic interests with affiliated faculty.

Do other Berkeley faculty help advise residents?

Yes. Beyond the Principal and Dean, a number of Cal faculty have been involved with Bowles for years and help support residents.

Community

What type of student succeeds most in a residential college?

Students who want to reach their academic goals, grow their social and leadership skills, and graduate career-ready. They embrace all kinds of diversity, get involved inside and outside the Hall, and want to contribute to their community.

What is Bowles's goal for gender diversity?

We don't consider gender, disability or other prohibited questions in selection. Bowles seeks a diverse community representative of Berkeley's undergraduate population.

Is Bowles open to all class years?

Yes. Bowles welcomes students across all undergraduate years and majors.

Do responsibilities evolve with each year?

Staying through graduation is strongly encouraged and brings more responsibility. Every resident can help lead events and the civic life of the Hall, upperclass students guide younger ones, and the Student President sits on the Foundation's Board of Directors.

Can residents join other Cal organizations, like Greek life?

Yes, and they're encouraged to. A residential college is meant to enhance the undergraduate experience, not replace it.

Can parents contribute to the endowment?

Absolutely, and we'd be grateful. The Bowles Hall Foundation is a 501(c)(3) and contributions are tax-deductible. See Giving for how to donate.

Applying

When does the application process take place?

Application periods differ for continuing Berkeley students, incoming transfers and newly admitted first-years. See Apply for details.

How are residents selected?

Essay answers go to a Selection Committee of current Bowles students, who never see demographic or background details. Each member scores responses on a set scale, and the averages determine Accepted or Waitlist status for each application period. Applicants are notified by email. We support the Fair Housing Act, which prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability or familial status.

I was waitlisted. Is the list ranked?

No, though only a limited number of students are waitlisted. Admission from the waitlist considers the Hall's goals of a rough balance of men and women, a mix of class years, and residents of varied backgrounds and interests.

Do you offer summer residency?

Yes, for individuals and groups during all summer sessions. Contact us to inquire.

Rooms & residency

How are rooms and roommates assigned?

The Hall has 188 beds in 42 singles and 73 doubles, each with an attached bathroom; 11 singles and 10 doubles are ADA-compliant and reserved for students with mobility or communication disabilities. We try to meet single or double requests, but assign rooms based on the needs of the community as a whole.

Incoming applicants share living-style preferences (cleanliness, quiet, bedtime) and can name roommates they'd like. We use a “best match” method to pair roommates. All rooms are single sex.

Can I change rooms or roommates?

Yes. The process is described in the Bowles Hall Community Standards Handbook, provided during the lease process.

Do returning students get priority for rooms?

Every room has the same modern amenities and an attached bathroom, but in a historic building not all rooms are alike: some are closer to dining or have bigger windows and better views. After accommodating demonstrated special needs, room selection gives priority to seniority in the Hall.

What about students who study abroad?

They keep their place in the community. We try to return them to the same room (and roommate) but can't guarantee it. We also work with the UC Education Abroad Program to host inbound exchange students at Bowles.

Is Bowles open over Thanksgiving and winter and spring breaks?

Yes, the Hall stays open, but there is no meal service.

Can continuing students store belongings over the summer?

There is some very limited summer storage for continuing students, and similar storage for students leaving temporarily to study abroad.
